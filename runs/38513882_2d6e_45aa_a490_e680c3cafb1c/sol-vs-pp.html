Prosper or Perish · run comparison · EU5 1.4

Is the economy overheating?

Partly. With Standard of Living there is far more money per person, but the AI builds the same buildings. What costs the people is food: each person eats 15–20 % more, the stores hold 3–5 fewer months until about 1550, and PP's stored-food growth bonus shrinks with them. That alone explains the 30 % fewer people by 1830.

Population: the gap opens before 1500

The SOL game already had 4 % fewer people at its first save in 1357. It then grows slower in almost every 20-year step until about 1590, and at the same pace as the PP runs afterwards. By 1827 it has 732 M people against 1,024 M and 1,084 M.

Investment and money: same buildings, more gold

This is where the overheating can be seen, and where it can't. The building charts lie on top of each other: the AI builds no more in absolute terms. The extra money shows in the tax base, which is the value of what gets made. It grows with prices, not with buildings.

What pops want: SOL moves demand off staples

From the first save, pops in the SOL game want 60 % more non-staple goods per person and a third fewer staple foods. That is what lifts prices and the tax base. The share of laborers and burghers rises because the peasants fall behind, not because there are more jobs.

Food: eating outruns making

The SOL game makes more food per person, with more farm levels per person, but eating rises faster. Two things add up. From the first save each pop eats 6–8 % more than a pop of the same type in the PP runs, 9–12 % more by 1512, in provinces with a low and a high SOL modifier alike. And as the share of laborers and burghers grows, food per person rises with it (+4 % by 1432, +7 % by 1512). Stores fill from the difference between making and eating, so they sit 3–5 months lower.

Prices: dear victuals early, cheap staples always

Victuals get dearer earlier in the SOL game, from the 1370s to the 1430s, then follow the PP runs. Staple foods stay 10–25 % cheaper because pops buy fewer of them. Other goods run 5–25 % dearer.

What explains the 30 % fewer people

PP's growth comes from stored food: the more months of eating a province holds (up to 24), the faster it grows. The engine reports that bonus for every province in every save. Comparing the bonus with the growth that actually happened splits the gap in two.

The chain, as the saves show it

  1. Pops eat more. From the first save, each pop eats 6–8 % more than a pop of the same type in the PP runs, 9–12 % more by 1512. It is a little higher where SOL's demand modifier is larger, but present everywhere. The saves don't name the modifier, and Standard of Living is the only known difference.
  2. Stores sit lower. Food made per person is higher too, but not enough: 3–5 fewer months in store from 1357 to about 1550, 1–2 fewer until about 1720.
  3. Growth slows. A smaller stored-food bonus means about 0.15 %/yr less growth in that period. Over the run that is the whole gap. Plague, wars and SOL's own growth modifiers (on a few hundred locations) add up to about zero.
  4. The mix tilts and feeds back. Peasants fall behind while the workers follow the buildings, so the hungrier pop types gain share, food per person rises further, and the stores stay low. Around 1650 the Little Ice Age drains every run's stores alike.
  5. Money is a side effect, not the driver. SOL's demand lifts prices and the tax base, but not construction. Victuals tighten early (1372–1432) and then level out.

Key numbers

PP + SOL against the two PP runs (77768726 / 16fe282a). The SOL game's last save is 1842, the PP runs end in 1837.

How this was measured

  • One SOL game against two PP runs. The two PP runs agree closely, so gaps much larger than their spread are likely real. One game cannot show how much the SOL game would vary itself.
  • The SOL game ran a PP build from GitHub (2 November or later), the PP runs builds from 3–5 November. Those builds differ in a few balance numbers (raw-material output, start market stock, trade). The two PP runs bracket them and still agree with each other.
  • Standard of Living's files were not available. The saves show its location modifiers (a pop-demand modifier and a migration modifier on every location, low- and high-growth modifiers on a few hundred). Which one raises food use is inferred, not read.
  • "Food eaten" is the engine's real consumption, worked back from its stored-food growth term (stored food ÷ yearly consumption, capped at two years). "Base rate" is PP's food use per pop type without modifiers.
  • Building capital prices every run's building levels at one price list (the SOL game's build), so it counts buildings, not prices. Money sums count countries that own land, without the Pope.
  • The SOL game has a save every 15–20 years, the PP runs every 5. Growth is compared over the same steps.
